Inside the Trading Desk

Trading Agents is a desk of twelve AI agents that studies a stock the way a trading firm would. This page shows how it works, end to end: the data it reads, who does what, where everything is kept, and how your dashboard and Robinhood fit in.

  1. Twelve agents research

    They study a stock from free public data, debate it, and propose a rated call.

  2. You see it beside your accounts

    The call, its reasoning and your Robinhood accounts, on your own dashboard.

  3. Only your Confirm trades

    One check by Robinhood, one Confirm by you. The agents never trade.

The whole picture

The desk on one page

Information flows from left to right. Public data goes in, the agents turn it into a call, the call is kept on your computer, and you decide on the dashboard. Robinhood is reached by the dashboard alone.

Where the information comes from

Seven sources, all public but one

Six free public sources feed the agents, with no keys and no accounts. The seventh, Robinhood, is yours and private: only the dashboard reads it. A source that does not answer is reported as unavailable, never as "nothing happened".

Yahoo Finance

Prices and news

Daily prices for charts and indicators, and company and world news. It gives its company and fund data only to web browsers, and the desk keeps its own name, so that data is mostly unavailable.

Prices can be up to 15 minutes late. It has no official free service: the desk reads the public pages its website uses, for personal use.

StockTwits

Traders' posts

What traders are saying about the stock right now, with their own bullish or bearish tags.

Recent posts only, so a past date gets few.

Reddit

Investor forums

Recent posts from wallstreetbets, stocks and investing; for a coin, from CryptoCurrency and CryptoMarkets.

Recent posts only.

FRED

The St. Louis Fed's economic data

Interest rates, bond yields, inflation, jobs and growth, for the US and the euro area.

For a past date, only figures that were already published by then.

Polymarket

Prediction-market odds

The crowd's priced chance of events ahead: rate decisions, recession, elections, conflicts and crypto.

Live odds only, so a past date gets none.

SEC EDGAR

Company filings

A US company's accounts exactly as filed, its shares and dividends for its valuation, its industry, and every trade its insiders report (Form 4). Each is dated by its filing, so a past day reads what was known then.

The SEC asks every reader for a contact email, so the desk sends yours, to the SEC alone.

Robinhood

Private, and yours

Your accounts: value, cash, buying power, holdings, live prices, orders and whether trades need your phone's approval. Read by the dashboard alone, after you connect it yourself. The agents see only the holdings the dashboard shares with them.

The role of each agent, and who does what

Twelve agents in five stations

The desk is laid out like a trading firm. Each station hands its work to the next, and each agent saves its part where the others can read it. The four analysts, who read at the same time, tell each other what to weigh (at most two short messages each, noted in their reports), and anyone stuck asks the lead instead of guessing. A lead runs the line.

trading-desk, the lead

The team you put to work in Madav / Claude. It plans each analysis, calls each agent in turn and checks it saved its work, writes the report, records the call, scores past calls, runs the morning and evening routines, and opens the dashboard. It has no order tool.

Each agent's part in one call, start to finish

One call, from question to order

Thirteen steps in three parts: the desk researches, you decide, and the desk learns. Choose a depth below to see how the two debates grow.

Depth of the analysis

1 turn each. The desk's setting today.

The desk researches

Steps 1 to 9. A few minutes, more at depth.
  1. 1

    You ask

    You

    In Madav / Claude: ask the team ("analyse NVDA"), or type /analyse NVDA in a chat. Or from a morning routine, or on the dashboard: press the robot beside a stock and choose how deep. It runs by itself when Madav or Claude Code is listening.

    Why it matters. Nothing starts on its own. A routine runs only because you switched it on, and an analysis from the dashboard only because you asked for it.

  2. 2

    Past calls are scored first

    The lead

    Any call whose five trading days are up is scored against its market's index, and gets a short lesson.

    Why it matters. Today's analysis starts from the desk's own track record.

  3. 3

    The plan

    The lead

    Which analysts run, how many turns, the report's language, your holdings as the dashboard last read them, and the lessons. The first time, it asks for your email for the SEC.

    Why it matters. Every agent works from the same brief, so a run can stop and resume where it left off.

  4. 4

    Four analysts gather the facts

    AnalystsFree sources

    Market

    Prices, indicators

    Sentiment

    Headlines, posts

    News

    News, economy, odds

    Fundamentals

    Accounts, or a fund's holdings

    Why it matters. All four run at the same time. Their reports are the evidence the rest of the desk argues from.

  5. 5

    The research debate

    BullBearResearch manager

    Why it matters. Bull speaks first, bear answers, turn by turn. The manager then writes the investment plan.

  6. 6

    The trade proposal

    Trader

    Entry, stop and size, from the plan, the checked price table and your holdings.

    Why it matters. The plan becomes something specific enough to test.

  7. 7

    The risk debate

    AggressiveConservativeNeutral

    Why it matters. Three temperaments challenge the trade, in that order, turn by turn.

  8. 8

    The call

    Portfolio manager

    A rating on five steps. Under each, what the dashboard's ticket will propose for it.

    SellSell everything held
    UnderweightSell half of what is held
    HoldNo order
    OverweightBuy half a position
    BuyBuy a full position: 10% of the account

    Why it matters. One clear word that the whole desk stands behind, with its reasons in the report.

  9. 9

    Recorded

    The leadRecords

    The complete report, a page per agent, and the call in the journal. The dashboard shows it straight away.

    Why it matters. You can read every step of the reasoning before you decide anything.

You decide

Steps 10 to 12. On the dashboard, when you choose.
  1. 10

    You open a ticket

    YouDashboard

    Decide… on the call proposes an order for the Agentic account, sized from the rating and checked against your caps. Or Trade…, beside any stock's price, opens your own ticket: buy or sell, starting at one share, under the very same checks.

    $500 an order at most10% of the account in one stock30% in one sector3 orders a dayOff after a 5% fall in a weekHeld at least 5 trading daysLimit orders, good for the day

    Why it matters. Your own limits stand between a call and an order. You set them on the dashboard, beside the trading switch, and they are kept in your private folder, where no agent can change them.

  2. 11

    Robinhood checks it

    DashboardRobinhood

    Review with Robinhood asks for Robinhood's own dry run of that exact order. Nothing is placed.

    Why it matters. Robinhood's alerts come back in its own words before any money moves.

  3. 12

    You confirm

    YouRobinhood

    Only with trading switched on, by you: Place order…, then the button naming the exact order, such as "Confirm: Buy 1 NVDA at $182.40". Robinhood can ask on your phone too. The dashboard logs what it sent and what Robinhood answered.

    Why it matters. This is the only way an order is ever placed. Seven checks guard it: see the Robinhood chapter.

The desk learns

Step 13. Five trading days later.
  1. 13

    Scored and learned

    The lead

    The call's return is compared with its market's index, such as SPY for a US stock, and a lesson is written. The scorecard on the dashboard updates.

    The lesson goes into step 2 of the next analysis.
The routines run this same line for you. The morning routine scores past calls, then analyses what is waiting and your starred stocks (at most five), and lists the new calls to decide on. The evening wrap-up scores what is ready and reports the day's calls and orders. Neither places an order.

How the information is stored

Four places, all on this computer

The desk keeps nothing in the cloud. The records live in the project folder, your rules in one settings file, your Robinhood sign-in, trading switch and caps in a private folder of your own, and the agents where Madav / Claude reads them.

This computer

The desk's records

In the project's desk-data folder. Never uploaded to GitHub.

  • JournalEvery call and its rating; once scored, its return against the index and its lesson.
  • ReportsA folder per analysis: a page per agent and the complete report.
  • WatchlistYour stocks, and which ones you starred.
  • Analyses you asked forEach stock you asked for on the dashboard, at the depth you chose: waiting, being analysed, done with its rating, or failed with why.
  • HoldingsWhat you hold, as the dashboard last read it from Robinhood.
  • Orders logEach order the dashboard sent, from a call or your own, and what Robinhood answered.
  • Your dashboardWhere you left it: the page, the stock, the chart, the stocks you looked at last. Never an order.
  • Your emailFor the SEC, and only the SEC.
  • Paper moneyWhat your paper record starts with ($10,000 as shipped). The record itself is worked out from the journal and closing prices each time.
  • Back-testsKept in journals of their own, apart from your real calls.

Your rules

The project's desk-settings.json. Edit it freely.

  • AnalysisWhich analysts, how many debate turns, the report's language.
  • ScoringFive trading days, against the index of the stock's market.
  • Position sizeA full position is 10% of the account. Your caps are not here: they are in your private folder, beside the trading switch.
  • RoutinesAt most five analyses a morning.
  • Safety netA value out of range falls back to the shipped one, never to a larger one.

Your private folder

In your home folder, outside the project. Agents are refused it; on Windows it is locked to your own account.

  • Robinhood sign-inKept after you connect on Robinhood's own page. The dashboard never sees your password.
  • Chosen accountsWhich accounts to show, by a fingerprint rather than their numbers, made with a secret kept here.
  • Trading switch, caps and guard railsOff when shipped. Only you turn it on, on the dashboard. Beside it: $500 an order, 10% in one stock, 30% in one sector, 5% in crypto, 3 orders a day, off by itself after a 5% fall in a week, and a 5-day shortest hold, as shipped.
  • Account valuesThe Agentic account's value, day by day for two weeks, for the weekly loss limit. Disconnect forgets it.
  • Dashboard keyWhile the dashboard runs, so that opening it again opens a page that works.

Madav / Claude

Madav's Agent Studio and scheduler, or Claude in this project.

  • The agentsThe twelve agents and the lead, read by Madav / Claude from the project's agents folder.
  • The teamIts line-up, shipped beside the lead for Madav's Teams tab; if you change it there, yours wins. In Claude, the lead calls the same twelve agents.
  • Reset the team"Reset the team" writes the agents' files again as shipped, and Madav goes back to them too, undoing changes made in Agent Studio; your model and permissions stay.
  • RoutinesThe project ships three to Madav, each off until you switch it on: run when the dashboard asks, the morning routine (8:00 on weekdays) and the evening wrap-up (17:00). An update to the desk pauses them until you switch them on again. In Claude, you start them with /routine.

Never kept anywhere: your full account numbers. The dashboard shows only their last four characters.

How it connects to Robinhood

Three narrow doors

Robinhood offers an agent connection with many tools. The desk uses it through three doors only, all inside your dashboard. Each door can do one thing, and the agents cannot reach any of them.

The agents

Twelve agents and their lead. They read the holdings the dashboard shares, and nothing more.

  • No broker or order tool
  • No Robinhood site
  • No sign-in folder
  • No dashboard address
refused by the guard

The read door

Only reads: your accounts, value, cash, buying power, holdings, live prices, orders and the phone-approval setting. Both accounts.

The check door

Only asks for Robinhood's dry run of one order. Nothing is placed.

The place door your Confirm

Only places one limit order, in the Agentic account, after every check below, whether the ticket came from a call (Decide…) or is your own (Trade…).

Robinhood

Agentic account

The only account the desk can trade, and only on your Confirm. You fund it yourself.

Main account

View only. The desk never trades here.

With phone approval on, Robinhood asks you once more before an order reaches the market.

Connecting is yours to doOn the dashboard you press Connect Robinhood and sign in on Robinhood's own page. The sign-in is kept in your private folder; the dashboard never sees your password, and Disconnect forgets it.

Before the place door opens, all of these must hold

  • You switched trading on yourself, after seeing the desk's record on paper. It ships off.
  • Robinhood checked this exact order with no alerts, less than five minutes ago.
  • Nothing in the order changed since that check.
  • Every cap passes again, on figures just read from Robinhood.
  • You pressed Confirm on the words of that exact order.
  • One order per ticket, with Robinhood's own guard against a repeat.
  • The order is for the Agentic account, never your main one.

And finally, the dashboard

Where you decide

A page that runs on your own computer, in the Madav House theme. The team opens it for you when you ask ("open the dashboard"). Your account sits at the top of its side list, which folds away to give the page the whole width. Its shape is shown here without figures; yours fills it with real ones.

Your desk today

Four tiles at the top: your accounts with their gain since bought, Agentic buying power, the S&P 500 today, and the desk's calls.

Start here

First on the page, broadest first: the whole market (four funds, yours to change), the eleven sectors in plain words, and your own names, each with today's move and the desk's latest call.

Markets, chart and stock page

Six market tiles; any stock or fund across the whole width, with averages and volume; and its own page: what you hold, your orders on it, each call and its score.

Watchlist and analyses

Grouped by tier, each folding away. The robot asks the desk to analyse a stock or a fund, as deep as you choose; each shows waiting, the step it is on, its rating or why it failed.

The desk's calls

Each call in two lines; in full, a coloured summary, the price since the call and every agent's part as a card.

Your paper record

Every call on a stock, a fund, Bitcoin or Ether followed on paper at its ticket's size, against the S&P 500, and how each call scored. Crypto stays on paper, within your crypto limit.

Your account

In the side list and on its own page: value, cash, buying power and gain in colour, each holding's share, orders, the trading switch and your caps.

Order tickets

Decide… from a call, or Trade… for your own: checked against your caps, reviewed by Robinhood, placed only on your Confirm.

AI can be wrong

The agents can misread data, miss news or reason badly, and can sound sure when they are wrong. Free data sources can be late, incomplete or wrong.

The past is no promise

Scores, track records and back-tests describe the past. They do not predict or guarantee future results.
